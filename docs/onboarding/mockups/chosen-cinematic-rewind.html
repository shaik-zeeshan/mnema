The signature — and where it is not allowed

Bookend motion

Motion lives on two screens only: Welcome and the Finale. Those are the bookends — the two frames that are pure product statement rather than decision-making, where nobody is reading a number or weighing an option. The six screens in between are working screens, and movement on a working screen is friction, so they are still: no scrub between them, no travelling playhead, nothing ambient. What they keep is functional feedback only — a download bar that is genuinely reporting bytes, a level meter that is genuinely reporting sound, and focus states. Three motions survive, all of them below, all of them things the product actually does.

01

The Rewind — Welcome, and the whole argument

A filmstrip of captured screens travelling backwards under a fixed playhead: a slow creep for six seconds, a hard snap back six frames, then a settle. Two ghost strips lag the live one by 100 and 200 ms, so the snap smears into a trail of frames instead of cutting — that is what dragging a playhead through video looks like, and it is the only gesture this product has. The timecode falls with it, two seconds per frame, which is the real default capture rate rather than a decorative number. When the strip settles, the frame sitting under the playhead gives up the words it was carrying — the promise (rewind) and the payoff (it read them) in one ten-second sentence, before a single permission has been asked for. Everything is keyed to the same 10 s cycle, so it reads as one gesture and not four loops sharing a screen. CSS only, no script.

Where my own judgement goes

Alternates

Five proposals, each rendered. Three things were tried and cut on the way here: the projected-disk curve, the proportional cost stack, and the measured/estimated chip pair — all of them information about the numbers rather than the numbers, and all of them density.

The weakest frame above is now 03, Capture & Storage. It is down to four lines, but it still carries four unrelated decisions and both hard gates, and it is the one screen a user cannot revisit meaningfully — by the time they understand what the rate means they have already recorded at it. Alternate 05 inverts it. Frame 05, Change settings, is a close second, and Alternate 02 attacks that one.

ALT 01Move both hard gates onto Welcome as a pre-flight, so no decision screen can ever refuse you

Neither hard gate is about a choice the user made — “is this folder writable” and “is there 729 MB free” are facts about the machine, and the machine knows both of them before the user has typed anything. Checking them on Welcome means the two blockers are resolved before a minute of work is invested, rather than after, and Capture & Storage becomes gate-free. The flow then has a property that is very easy to explain to a user and to a founder: no screen where you make a decision can ever refuse to let you leave it. The cost is that Welcome stops being purely a bookend, and the pre-flight has to stay quiet enough not to dilute the promise.

ALT 02Delete the Change settings screen — make it a mode of the same document

Your settings and Change settings show the same eight things twice, in two different component vocabularies, on two screens the user has to navigate between. Make it one document with a read mode and an edit mode: the rows keep their positions, the controls appear in place of the values, and nothing else moves — this is a working screen, so the change of mode is instant rather than performed. It removes a screen and removes the “does going back re-resolve the totals?” question entirely, because there is no back. The tension: the brief says Review must not be a form. This does not make it one — no row expands, nothing discloses — but the whole document does change state, and that is a decision worth taking deliberately rather than by accident.

ALT 03Setup is not a step. It is a strip that follows you.

The Setup screen's entire design problem is that it must not block — every requirement on it is a requirement that it get out of the way. A screen whose job is to be leavable probably should not be a screen. Press Start and land directly on Voice, with the downloads pinned as a strip along the bottom that persists into the Finale and then into the app. The user never has to decide to leave, because they were never stopped; the byte-weighted aggregate, the per-item errors and the cancel-turns-the-feature-off rule all survive intact inside the strip, one click away. Eight steps become seven, and the step that existed to be skipped is gone.

ALT 04End on the gesture, not on a still — the Finale hands you the scrubber

The Finale currently proves that Mnema captured something. ALT 05Turn the rate slider around: let people choose the disk, and derive the rate from it

Nobody wants a snapshot interval. They want to know what this will cost them, and the interval is only how the app achieves it — so the slider on Capture & Storage is currently pointed at the wrong quantity. Label it in megabytes a day, and show the interval as the derived readout underneath. It fixes the honesty problem at the same time: an estimate is a much more comfortable thing to set than to be quoted, and the one measured point on the ladder can be marked on the scale where it will actually be read. The risk is that it promises a budget the app cannot enforce — a busy day is a busy day — so the number has to stay written as “about”, and a hard cap would be a different feature.
