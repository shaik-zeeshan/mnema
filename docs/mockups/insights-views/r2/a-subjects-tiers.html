02How it behaves

  1. 01Open SubjectsAll four tiers are on screen, 10 subjects each, in reading order. With the Young dossier every tier fits, so no stepper shows — just the counts.
  2. 02Turn one tier‹ › in a tier's heading, or ←/→ while the pointer (or the row you're on) is in that tier. Only that tier's rows change; its heading stays exactly where it was and the other tiers keep their pages.
  3. 03More per pageClick "11–20 of 69" for a tiny menu: 10 / 25 / 50 / Show all. The first row you were looking at stays on the page.
  4. 04Look at FadingIt starts folded to its heading and a "Show N fading" link. Unfolded, it pages like the others, and "Hide" folds it again.
  5. 05FilterTyping in the search box swaps the tiers for one "Matches · 23" section with the same pager. Clear it and every tier is back on the page it was on.
  6. 06Open a subject, come backClick a row (or ↑/↓ then Enter). Esc or the crumb brings you back to the same scroll spot, every tier on its page, and the row you opened marked.

03Trade-offs

Good at

Keeps the app's core reading — strongest to faintest — intact at every size: no tier can be pushed off the first screen by another. The pager is part of the heading, in the heading's own type, so it reads as Mnema rather than a web table, and it disappears when a tier fits. Each tier remembers its page, so coming back from a subject is exactly where you left.

Costs

Four little pagers instead of one: there's no "page 3 of the whole list", and jumping far inside a big tier takes several ‹ › clicks (or "Show all"). With a very large Strongly held tier on 25 or 50 per page, the tiers below need a long scroll. The chevrons are small (22px) on purpose, which makes them a smaller target than 1-A's buttons.
