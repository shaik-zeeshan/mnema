Numbered pages — one pager per list, and it always says where you are

Today's Subjects tab and subject page, unchanged, except how long lists page. Today each tier has its own "Show 10 more / Show less", the map has its own "Show all 138", conclusions have another, and the evidence sits in a box that scrolls by itself. Here every list gets exactly one small pager — "‹ 1 2 3 … 7 › 21–40 of 138" — and tier names become dividers inside it. A subject opens as two side-by-side panes that never scroll the page: conclusions on the left, the chosen one's evidence on the right, each with its pager pinned at its foot, so the row you picked and its details are always on screen together.

02How it behaves

  1. 01Open SubjectsTiers are dividers inside one list. With the Young dossier everything fits, so there is no pager at all.
  2. 02Turn pagesClick a number, ‹ ›, or press ←/→. The pager stays under your pointer; "21–40 of 138" always says where you are. Changing 20/50/100 keeps your row.
  3. 03Open a subjectClick a row or press Enter. A slim strip holds the name, four numbers and a small chart; below it two panes fill the window and the page itself never scrolls. Conclusions page 10 at a time on the left.
  4. 04Pick a conclusionClick a row or press ↑/↓; the right pane follows and starts at its top. Page the list away and the right pane keeps your pick; "selected: page 3" jumps back. In a narrow window the list folds into a picker with ‹ ›.
  5. 05Page its evidence8 entries per page, newest first, pager pinned at the bottom of the right pane. Press E (or click the pane) so ←/→ turns the evidence; C hands them back to the list. Neither pane ever moves the other.
  6. 06Go backEsc or the crumb lands you on the page holding the subject you opened, with it marked.

03Trade-offs

Good at

Most predictable of the five: one look tells you the size of the list and your place in it. Jumping far is one click (1 … 7). The subject page never scrolls as a whole: the chosen row and its evidence sit in two panes, each with its own pager, so one can't push the other off screen. The same pager works for 10 items or 10,000.

Costs

A tier is cut into pages ("Forming (cont.)"), so you can't see a whole tier at once unless you pick 100 per page. On a subject, the big title, four large numbers and the 200-pixel chart shrink to a one-line strip with a small chart, and list rows clip long statements to two lines (the full text is in the right pane). Page numbers shift as new subjects form, so "page 3" is only stable for a while.

What changes in the app. subjectsTiers.ts: pageOf ("Show N more / Show less") becomes a page-slice helper that returns the rows for page N plus the "21–40 of 138" numbers; TIER_PAGE becomes the default page size, CONCLUSION_PAGE drops to 10, and an evidence page size of 8 is added. Subjects.svelte: the per-tier pagers go; the tiers render as dividers inside one paged list with one shared pager, and the page is remembered when you open a subject and come back. ConvictionMap.svelte: "Show all 138" goes — the map stays the top 12 with a "Go to the list" link. SubjectDetail.svelte: the grid with its sticky, inner-scrolling side column becomes two fixed-height panes that fill the window, each with the same pager pinned at its foot; the big header and stats become a slim strip, and in a narrow window the conclusions list becomes a picker above the details. SubjectChart.svelte gets a small strip-sized version.
