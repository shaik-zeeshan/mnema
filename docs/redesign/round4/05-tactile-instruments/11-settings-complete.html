Page 11  Settings, whole — five toolbar tabs, nineteen sections, every real row

Pages 05 and 06 drew the shell and the showcase. This one draws everything: every group and every row that actually exists in settings-index.ts and settings/panels/**, in the app's own order, with nothing invented. Each tab is rendered as one continuous column — the real window is 1100 × 720 and scrolls, but a scrolling frame cannot prove completeness, so the window is allowed to grow here. The count that matters: seven instruments across the whole app, and they are marked. Everything else — well over a hundred controls — is a stock switch, popup, segmented control, radio group, slider, stepper or field, and that ratio is the direction.

Tab 2 — Capture · Capture, Video, Audio, Privacy

Four sections and three instruments — the densest tab in the app for physical consequence. Segment length prices a crash. Capture rate prices a day. The retention ladder prices what survives. Everything between them is a switch, a radio group or a slider, because a sensitivity percentage has no unit you can spend. Retention is drawn here, under Privacy, not under Data — this direction's standing deviation: the ladder belongs beside the thing it culls. Data › Storage keeps a pointer row so the group still exists where the index puts it.

Tab 3 — Intelligence · Providers, MCP, Ask AI, User Context, OCR, Transcription, Speakers, Semantic Search

Eight sections, forty-five rows, three instruments. That ratio is the whole argument: the model picker, the OCR duty meter and the semantic-index gauge earn a machined face because each one spends RAM, cycles or database bytes and can say how much against this Mac. The forty-two rows around them — every provider field, every consent disclosure, every connector, every status line — are stock. Three consent gates are drawn as their real disclosure blocks rather than bare switches, because the switch is not the setting: the sentence under it is.

The two short tabs, side by side. Data holds exactly two groups — where the files live, and who else is allowed to read them. Agent access lives here, not under Intelligence: the CLI broker is a grant list over your own history, while MCP connectors are tools the model may call, and the app keeps them on opposite sides of that line. About is version, license and logs; the license row is the one place in Mnema that counts devices, and it counts them without ever naming one.

The count, out loud. Ninety-six indexed rows, nineteen sections, five tabs, plus twenty-one shortcut captures the index does not number. Seven of those carry a machined face — shortcut recorder, segment length, capture rate, retention, OCR duty, model picker, semantic index. Everything else is stock. Any eighth has to answer the same question the other seven do: name the physical quantity, and write the consequence as a fraction of something real on this Mac. A sensitivity slider cannot. A cache duration cannot. A theme cannot. They stay rows.
