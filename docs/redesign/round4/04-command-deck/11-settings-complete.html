04 · Command Deck Page 11 — Settings, complete

All 96 rows, five tabs, one scroll each — and nothing invented to fill a gap.

Pages 05 and 06 showed the settings shape. This page is the census: every real group and every real row the app registers, in the five ⌃-tabs this direction replaced the sidebar with. Each tab below is its full scroll unrolled — one 1100px-wide window with its tab strip on top and its deck on the bottom, drawn at whatever height its content needs. Conditional rows carry a when … badge naming what gates them, and the rows the app can compute a real quantity for carry that quantity in the accent colour. The count in the deck is real: the row index has exactly 96 entries and a test fails the build when a registered setting is missing from it.

Where the G-decisions outranked the mockups

Five places this census deliberately does not match pages 05 and 06

Pages 05 / 06 drawThis pageRule
Retention ladder 7d · 30d · 90d · 1y · ∞ 7 days · 14 days · 30 days · Forever G8. 90d and 1y do not exist in the retention policy type. A denominator over an option that cannot be chosen is worse than no denominator.
A ⌘Z Undo hint in the settings deckNot drawn. G7. Settings-Undo is out for v1. The row echo and the deck's timestamped state answer the anxiety; an undo stack is speculative machinery.
“⌘⇧A is taken by CleanShot X”“This shortcut is taken by another app — try a different combination” G9. macOS has no API to enumerate another app's hotkeys. Naming one would be a guess printed as a fact. In-app conflicts are named, and this page draws that case too.
OCR duty bar with a thermal claimBoth duty halves, split in seconds, and the real backlog count. G8. No °C claims anywhere. The row also states it is reported, not configurable — the two duty cycles are governor constants.
A semantic coverage meter, always onOff state states the price; the meter only exists once it is enabled. G10. Shipping default is off with zero embeddings, so a coverage bar reading 0% would be a meter of nothing. The price is computed from the real frame count.
Structural notes

Four things the census turned up that are worth deciding on, not just drawing

FindingWhat it means
Intelligence carries 46 of 96 rows Nearly half the settings surface behind one tab, and ⌘F is doing most of the work of finding anything inside it. The tab is honest — these things really are one family — but it is the strongest argument this direction has for the filter being the real navigation rather than a convenience.
Eleven Capture rows are conditional Six of them on one toggle. The when … badge is drawn here as spec annotation; in the app they simply are not rendered. Worth keeping that way — a greyed row you cannot reach is worse than a row that is not there.
Deletes are scattered across four tabs Retention and cleanup in Data; Wipe User Context in Intelligence; delete-unused-model in three separate groups; delete voiceprint in a fourth; two log deletes in About; revoke-grant in Data. Nothing here proposes a “delete everything” pane — but a user asking “what does Mnema still have of mine?” has seven places to look.
The shortcut recorders are outside the index Deliberate: they are one row per registered action across four categories, so indexing them makes the 96 a moving number and the completeness test unfalsifiable. The cost is that ⌘F cannot find “Quick Access” by name — which is exactly the row a user is most likely to search for.
