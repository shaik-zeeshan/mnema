09 · Subjects — tiers by conviction, sparkline as the hero

Look at: a subject row is a trajectory, not a record. The sparkline is the widest, tallest, only drawn thing in the row — the number beside it is the sparkline's caption, not the other way round. Tiers are labels floating over the pane; the rows they hold sit on one opaque plate each, so a whole tier reads as a single card of the same material. The dashed line inside every sparkline is the 0.15 display floor, which is why the Fading tier's lines are drawn below it rather than deleted.

What the tile can honestly say

A “Subject” is not a backend row — it is a client-side group-by over Conclusion.subject. So the tile may show a subject's name, its top conclusion's confidence, and the active/fading split. It may not show an evidence count: the index never loads one.

The two tiers below the fold

A fading subject is not hidden and not deleted — it keeps its plate, loses its colour, and its trajectory is drawn crossing the dashed floor on the way down. That crossing is the whole idea of the tier, so it must be visible; a tier that just said “faded” in grey would throw away the one fact worth seeing.

Honesty audit — what this page refuses to draw

No subject-level score

There is no rolled-up subject confidence anywhere in the backend. The 0.72 on a row is literally its top conclusion's confidence, rendered toFixed(2) — which is why it never gains a progress bar of its own.

No evidence count on the index

The index row can only say N conclusions. “11 linked activities” appears on the detail, where the evidence refs have actually been resolved.

No edit, no rating, no “wrong”

Two commands exist: set_pinned and dismiss_conclusion. Dismiss is not delete — it raises a resurface bar to 2× the evidence that formed it, and the belief can re-form later.

The sparkline has no time axis

Snapshot points are evenly spaced by index, not by date — the backend stores a confidence history, not a time series. Drawing dates under it would be a lie, so there are none.
